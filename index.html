Questions Page Number 95

1. Why is diffusion insufficient to meet the oxygen requirements of multicellular organisms like humans?

Solution:

Multi-cellular organisms like humans have very big bodies and require a lot of oxygen to diffuse into the body quickly in order to meet the oxygen requirement. Diffusion is a slow process which will take a lot of time to circulate oxygen to all the body cells. Because of its slow nature, diffusion is insufficient to meet the oxygen requirements of multicellular organisms like humans.

2. What criteria do we use to decide whether something is alive?

Solution:

Walking, breathing, growth and other visible changes can be used to determine whether something is alive or dead. However, some living things will have changes that are not visible to our eye; Hence, the presence of the life process is a fundamental criterion to decide whether something is alive.

3. What are outside raw materials used for by an organism?

Solution:

The outside raw material is used by organisms for food and oxygen. Raw materials’ requirement varies on the complexity of the organism and the environment it is living.

4. What processes would you consider essential for maintaining life?

Solution:

Life processes such as respiration, digestion, excretion, circulation and transportation are essential for maintaining life.

3. What is the role of the acid in our stomach?

Solution:

HCl present in the stomach dissolves food particles and creates an acidic medium. In an acidic environment, protein-digesting enzymes, pepsinogen, are converted into pepsin. HCl in the stomach also acts as a protective barrier against many disease-causing pathogens.

4. What is the function of digestive enzymes?

Solution:

Digestive enzymes break complex food molecules into simpler ones. This will make the food absorption process easy and effective. Absorbed food is transported to all parts of the body by the blood.

5. How is the small intestine designed to absorb digested food?

Solution:

The small intestine has small projections called microvilli, which increase the surface volume, making the absorption more effective. Within the villi, there are numerous blood vessels that absorb digested food and carry it to the bloodstream. Blood transports food to each part of our body.

1. What advantage over an aquatic organism does a terrestrial organism have with regard to obtaining oxygen for respiration?

Solution:

Terrestrial organisms breathe by using atmospheric oxygen, whereas aquatic organisms take oxygen dissolved in water. The oxygen level is high in the atmosphere when compared to oxygen in the water. Hence, terrestrial organisms need not breathe fast to obtain organisms, whereas aquatic organisms need to breathe faster to get the required oxygen.

3. How are oxygen and carbon dioxide transported in human beings?

Solution:

Oxygen and Carbon dioxide are transported in human beings via the bloodstream. Oxygen is carried to the cells, whereas carbon dioxide is carried away from the cells. The exchange of gases takes place between the alveoli of the lungs and the surrounding blood capillaries.  Oxygen is absorbed by the blood capillaries from the lungs’ alveoli by diffusion, while carbon dioxide is absorbed by the lungs’ alveoli from the blood capillaries by diffusion.

4. How are the lungs designed in human beings to maximise the area for the exchange of gases?

Solution:

  • The lungs are an important part of the body. The passage inside the lungs divides into smaller and smaller tubes, which finally terminate in balloon-like structures called alveoli.
  • The alveoli provide a surface where the exchange of gases can take place. The walls of the alveoli usually contain an extensive network of blood vessels. We know that when we breathe in, we lift our ribs, flatten our diaphragm and the chest cavity becomes larger.
  • Because of this action, the air is sucked into the lungs and fills up the expanded alveoli.
  • The blood brings the essential carbon dioxide from the rest of the body and supplies it to the alveoli; the oxygen in the alveolar air is taken up by the blood in the alveolar blood vessels to be transported to all other cells of the body. During the normal breathing cycle, when air is taken in and let out, the lungs always contain a residual volume of air so that there is sufficient time for oxygen to be absorbed and carbon dioxide to be released.

2. Why is it necessary to separate oxygenated and deoxygenated blood in mammals and birds?

Solution:

Mammals and birds are warm-blooded animals which keep their body temperature constant irrespective of the environment they live. This process requires a lot of oxygen for more cellular respiration so that warm-blooded animals produce more energy to balance their body temperature. Hence, it is very important for warm-blooded animals to separate oxygenated and deoxygenated blood to keep their circulatory system efficient.

There are two types of conducting tissues in highly organised plants that carry out the transport system 1) Xylem 2) Phloem. Xylem conduct water and minerals from roots to the rest of the plant parts. Similarly, Phloem transports food materials from the leaf to other parts of the plant.

After this, there will be a steady movement of water from the root xylem to all parts of the plant connected through conducting interconnected water-conducting channels.

Structure of Nephron

Functioning of Nephron

  • The blood enters the kidney through the renal artery, which branches into many capillaries associated with the glomerulus.
  • The water and solute are transferred to the nephron at Bowman’s capsule.
  • In the proximal tubule, substances such as amino acids, glucose, and salts are selectively reabsorbed, and unwanted molecules are added to the urine.
  • The filtrate then moves down into the loop of Henle, where more water is absorbed. From here, the filtrate moves upwards into the distal tubule and finally to the collecting duct. The collecting duct collects urine from many nephrons.
  • The urine formed in each kidney enters a long tube called the ureter. From the ureter, it gets transported to the urinary bladder and then into the urethra.

2. What are the methods used by plants to get rid of excretory products?

Solution:

Plants can get rid of excess water by transpiration.

For other wastes, plants use the fact that many of their tissues consist of dead cells and that they can even lose some parts, such as leaves. Many plant waste products are stored in cellular vacuoles. Waste products may be stored in leaves that fall off.

Other waste products are stored as resins and gums, especially in old xylem. Plants also excrete some waste substances into the soil around them.

3. How is the amount of urine produced regulated?

Solution:

The amount of urine produced depends on the amount of excess water and dissolved waste present in the body. Other factors may be the environment and the ADH hormone, which regulates the production of urine.

8. What are the differences between aerobic and anaerobic respiration? Name some organisms that use the anaerobic mode of respiration.

Solution:

Aerobic respiration

  • The process takes place in the presence of free oxygen.
  • The products of aerobic respiration are CO2, water and energy.
  • The first step of aerobic respiration (glycolysis) takes place in the cytoplasm, while the next step takes place in mitochondria.
  • The process of aerobic respiration takes place in all higher organisms.
  • In this process, complete oxidation of glucose takes place.

Anaerobic respiration

  • The process takes place in the absence of free oxygen.
  • The products of anaerobic respiration are ethyl alcohol, CO2 and a little energy.
  • Even in anaerobic respiration, the first step takes place in the cytoplasm, while the next step takes place in mitochondria.
  • In this process, the glucose molecules are incompletely broken down.
  • The process of anaerobic respiration takes place in lower organisms like yeast, some species of bacteria and parasites like tapeworms.

11. Describe the double circulation of blood in human beings. Why is it necessary?

Solution:

Double circulation means, in a single cycle, blood goes twice in the heart. The process helps in separating oxygenated and deoxygenated blood to maintain a constant body temperature.

The double circulatory system of blood includes

  • Pulmonary circulation
  • Systemic circulation.

Pulmonary circulation

The right ventricle pumps deoxygenated blood into the lungs, where it is oxygenated. The oxygenated blood is brought back to the left atrium, and from there, it is pumped into the left ventricle. Finally, blood goes into the aorta for systemic circulation.

Systemic circulation

The oxygenated blood is pumped to various parts of the body from the left ventricle. The deoxygenated blood from different parts of the body passes through the vena cava to reach the right atrium. The right atrium transfers the blood into the right ventricle.

1. What is the difference between a reflex action and walking?

A reflex action is a quick, automatic and involuntary response to a stimulus. It protects the body from sudden harmful situations. It is generally coordinated through the spinal cord without conscious thinking. The sensory nerves carry the message to the spinal cord, and motor nerves immediately carry the response to the muscles. Walking is a voluntary action controlled by the brain. We decide when and where to walk, and the brain coordinates the movement of our muscles. Therefore, reflex action is rapid and automatic, whereas walking is usually conscious and controlled.

2. What happens at the synapse between two neurons?

When an electrical impulse reaches the end of one neuron, chemicals called neurotransmitters are released into the synaptic gap. These chemicals diffuse across the small gap and reach the next neuron. They stimulate the next neuron and start a new electrical impulse in it. The impulse therefore passes from one neuron to another in only one direction. This arrangement helps the nervous system transmit messages in an organised and controlled manner.

3. Which part of the brain maintains posture and equilibrium of the body?

The cerebellum is the part of the hindbrain that maintains posture, balance and equilibrium of the body. It coordinates the precise movements of skeletal muscles. It receives information from muscles, joints and the eyes about the position of the body. It compares this information and adjusts muscle activity when required. Thus, it helps us walk, write, cycle and perform other activities smoothly and accurately.

4. How do we detect the smell of agarbatti?

Odour molecules enter the nose and stimulate olfactory receptors present in the nasal passage. The receptors generate nerve impulses that travel through sensory neurons to the brain. The brain interprets these impulses as the smell of agarbatti. This process is important for proper coordination, quick response and normal functioning of the organism. It shows how different parts of the body work together according to the needs of the situation.

5. What is the role of the brain in reflex action?

Reflex actions are mainly coordinated by the spinal cord for a quick response. The brain receives information about the action through sensory pathways and becomes aware of it. The brain also helps in controlling and modifying reflex responses when necessary. This process is important for proper coordination, quick response and normal functioning of the organism. How Do Organisms Reproduce?

Class 10 Biology • Chapter 3

Textual Questions and Answers

Questions Page No. 128

1. What is the importance of DNA copying in reproduction?

DNA, or Deoxyribonucleic acid, is the genetic material present in the cells of all organisms. It carries the instructions and genetic information needed to form a new organism. During reproduction, a copy of the parent’s DNA is passed to the offspring, allowing the offspring to inherit characteristics from its parents. DNA copying also helps maintain the basic body design and features of a species. Small changes during copying may produce variations, and these variations can help organisms survive changing environmental conditions and may lead to the origin of new species.

2. Why is variation beneficial to the species but not necessarily for the individual?

Variation means differences between individuals of the same species. A particular variation may not always be useful to the individual possessing it, because it may not provide any immediate advantage. However, variations are very important for the survival of the species as a whole. If environmental conditions change suddenly, such as an increase in temperature or a shortage of food, some individuals may possess variations that help them survive. These surviving organisms reproduce and pass the useful variation to the next generation. Therefore, variation increases the chances of survival of the species even when it may not benefit every individual.

2. How will an organism be benefited if it reproduces through spores?

Spore formation is an advantageous method of reproduction because a large number of spores can be produced inside one sporangium. The spores are very light and can be carried to distant places by air, which reduces competition among the newly formed organisms. They are also surrounded by thick protective walls that prevent dehydration and protect them from unfavourable conditions. When suitable moisture, temperature and nutrients are available, the spores germinate and form new organisms. Thus, spores help organisms reproduce, spread and survive difficult conditions.

3. Can complex organisms give rise to new individuals through regeneration?

Highly complex organisms cannot usually produce complete new individuals through regeneration because their bodies are organised into specialised tissues, organs and organ systems. These systems are interconnected and perform different functions in coordination with one another. Such organisms may regenerate or repair certain damaged parts, such as skin, blood cells or muscles, but a complete organism cannot develop from a small body fragment. Regeneration in complex organisms is therefore mainly a process of repair and replacement rather than reproduction.

4. Why is vegetative propagation practised for growing some types of plants?

Vegetative propagation is practised because many plants, such as banana, orange and pineapple, do not produce viable seeds. It is a rapid, simple and economical method of producing a large number of plants. It can also be used when seed germination is difficult or when the seeds are not able to grow under particular conditions. The new plants are genetically similar to the parent plant, so useful qualities of a good variety can be preserved. This method is therefore widely used in agriculture and horticulture.

5. Why is DNA copying an essential part of reproduction?

DNA copying is essential because DNA contains the hereditary information of an organism. Before a cell divides, its DNA is copied so that each daughter cell receives a complete set of genetic instructions. Along with DNA, the cell also duplicates other cellular structures and then divides. DNA copying ensures continuity of body design and inherited characters from parents to offspring. Although copying is generally accurate, small changes may occur and produce variations, which are important for adaptation and evolution.

At puberty, girls show several physical and hormonal changes. Hair begins to grow in the genital region and underarms, and body hair may become more noticeable. The ovaries and uterus increase in size and begin functioning more actively. Breasts develop and the skin around the nipples becomes darker. Menstruation begins when the reproductive system becomes mature. Increased oil secretion from the skin may also cause pimples. These changes occur because of the action of hormones released during puberty.

4. How does the embryo get nourishment inside the mother’s body?

After fertilization, the lining of the uterus becomes thick and rich in blood vessels. The embryo gets attached to this lining through a special tissue called the placenta. The placenta contains finger-like projections called villi on the embryo’s side and blood spaces on the mother’s side. It provides a large surface area for the exchange of nutrients and oxygen. Food and oxygen pass from the mother’s blood to the embryo, while carbon dioxide and waste materials pass from the embryo to the mother’s blood for removal.

5. If a woman uses a Copper-T, will it protect her from sexually transmitted diseases?

No, Copper-T does not protect a woman from sexually transmitted diseases. It is an intrauterine contraceptive device that helps prevent pregnancy by making the uterus unsuitable for fertilization or implantation. It does not act as a barrier between the body fluids of two people. Therefore, infections such as HIV, syphilis and gonorrhoea can still be transmitted. Condoms are the contraceptive method that also help reduce the risk of sexually transmitted infections when used correctly.

Exercises Page No. 141

1. Asexual reproduction takes place through budding in

Answer: (b) Yeast. Yeast reproduces asexually by budding. A small outgrowth develops on the parent cell and grows gradually. The nucleus divides, and one daughter nucleus moves into the bud. The bud becomes larger and finally separates from the parent cell. Sometimes the buds remain attached and form a chain of cells. This process produces new yeast individuals without the fusion of gametes.

2. Which is not a part of the female reproductive system?

Answer: (c) Vas deferens. The vas deferens is a part of the male reproductive system. It is a muscular tube that carries mature sperms from the epididymis towards the urethra. The ovary, uterus and fallopian tube are parts of the female reproductive system. The fallopian tube carries the egg from the ovary and is also the usual site of fertilization. Therefore, vas deferens is not a female reproductive organ.

3. The anther contains

Answer: (d) Pollen grains. The anther is the male reproductive part of a flower and is present at the tip of the stamen. It contains pollen sacs in which pollen grains are produced. Pollen grains carry the male reproductive cells or male gametophyte of a seed plant. During pollination, they are transferred to the stigma. Thus, pollen grains are the correct answer.

4. What are the advantages of sexual reproduction over asexual reproduction?

Sexual reproduction involves the fusion of male and female gametes and combines genetic material from two parents. As a result, the offspring show variations and are not exact copies of either parent. These variations increase the ability of a population to adapt to environmental changes. Sexual reproduction also improves the chances of survival of the species over long periods. It is especially useful in changing environments because some offspring may possess favourable characteristics.

5. What are the functions performed by the testis?

The testes are the male reproductive organs in human beings. Their main function is to produce male gametes called sperms. They also secrete male sex hormones called androgens, especially testosterone. Testosterone is responsible for the development of secondary sexual characters such as deepening of the voice, growth of facial and body hair and development of muscles. The testes are located in the scrotum, where the temperature is slightly lower than the body temperature, which is necessary for sperm formation.

6. Why does menstruation occur?

Every month, an egg is released from one of the ovaries and the uterus prepares for a possible pregnancy. The inner lining of the uterus becomes thick and develops a rich supply of blood vessels so that it can nourish an embryo. If fertilization does not occur, the egg does not develop into an embryo. The thickened uterine lining is no longer required and breaks down gradually. Blood, mucus and tissue are then discharged through the vagina. This process is called menstruation and usually occurs once every month.

Use the labelled longitudinal-section-of-a-flower diagram provided with the chapter.

8. What are the different methods of contraception?

Contraception includes natural, barrier, oral, intrauterine and surgical methods. Natural methods avoid sexual contact during the fertile period of the menstrual cycle. Barrier methods such as male and female condoms, diaphragms and cervical caps prevent sperms from reaching the ovum. Oral contraceptive pills contain hormones that prevent ovulation or fertilization. Devices such as Copper-T are placed in the uterus to prevent fertilization or implantation. In surgical methods, the fallopian tubes in females or the vas deferens in males are blocked. These methods help prevent unplanned pregnancy, while condoms also reduce the risk of sexually transmitted infections.

9. How are modes of reproduction different in unicellular and multicellular organisms?

In unicellular organisms, the whole organism is a single cell, so reproduction generally occurs by division of that cell. Common methods include binary fission, multiple fission and budding. In multicellular organisms, different tissues and organs perform specialised functions, so reproduction may occur through vegetative propagation, spore formation or sexual reproduction. Simple multicellular organisms may reproduce by fragmentation or regeneration. Complex organisms such as humans and many animals reproduce mainly through sexual reproduction.

10. How does reproduction help in providing stability to populations?

Reproduction produces new individuals and maintains the number of organisms in a population. It replaces individuals that die due to age, disease, accidents or environmental causes. It also transfers hereditary information from one generation to the next and maintains the basic characteristics of a species. Variations produced during reproduction help populations adjust to changing conditions. A balance between the birth rate and death rate helps maintain population stability.

11. What could be the reasons for adopting contraceptive methods?

Contraceptive methods are adopted to prevent unplanned or unwanted pregnancies and to provide spacing between children. They help couples plan the size of their family according to their health, education and financial conditions. Contraception can reduce health risks related to closely spaced pregnancies. Barrier methods such as condoms also help protect against sexually transmitted infections. Thus, contraceptive methods support responsible parenthood, family planning and better health of individuals and families.

1. What is heredity?

Answer: Heredity is the transmission of characteristics from parents to offspring through genes.

2. What are genes?

Answer: Genes are units of heredity present on chromosomes. They control the expression of traits.

3. What is variation?

Answer: Variation means differences in characteristics among individuals of the same species.

4. Why are variations important?

Answer: Variations help organisms adapt to changing environments and form the basis of evolution.

5. State Mendel’s law of dominance.

Answer: When two contrasting alleles are present together, only one allele expresses itself in the hybrid; it is called the dominant allele.

6. What is evolution?

Answer: Evolution is the gradual change in inherited characteristics of populations over generations.

Page: 143

1. If a trait A exists in 10% of a population of an asexually reproducing species and trait B exists in 60% of the same population, which trait is likely to have arisen earlier?

Solution: Trait B is more probable to arise early as this trait has already been existing and replicating in a larger percentage of the population as compared to trait A.

2. How does the creation of variations in a species promote survival?

Solution: Genetic variations enable the species to better adapt to changes in its environment. Moreover, it is an important force in evolution as it allows the frequency of alleles to increase or decrease through natural selection. These variations will determine the difference between extinction or continuation of the species.

Exercise-9.2 Page: 147

1. How do Mendel’s experiments show that traits may be dominant or recessive?

Solution: Mendel showed that the traits could either be dominant or recessive through his experiments that focused on the mono-hybrid cross. The experiment involved him crossing tall (TT) pea plants with dwarf (tt) pea plants. The resultant plants which formed after fertilisation represented the F1 (or filial) generation. All the F1 plants were tall. Mendel then proceeded to self-pollinate the filial generation plants, and the result was that 1/4th of the plants obtained in the F2 generation were dwarfs. From this experiment, Mendel concluded that the F1 tall plants were not true-breeding; instead, they carried the traits for both tall and dwarf heights. A portion of the plants was tall due to the fact that the traits for tallness were dominant over the traits for dwarfness. This cements the notion that traits can either be dominant or recessive.

2. How do Mendel’s experiments show that traits are inherited independently?

Solution: Mendel’s experiments show that traits are inherited independently through his dihybrid cross experiment. The experiment involved him using two traits – namely, seed shape and seed colour. The colour yellow (YY) is dominant over green (yy), while the round shape (RR) is dominant over the wrinkled shape (rr). The F2 progeny of the dihybrid cross resulted in a phenotypic ratio of 9:3:3:1; therefore, 9 plants with round yellow (RRYY) seeds, 3 plants with round green (RRyy) seeds and 3 plants with wrinkled yellow (rrYY) seeds and one with wrinkled green seeds (rryy). He further observed that the wrinkled greens and the round yellow are parental combinations while the round green and wrinkled yellow are new. A dihybrid cross between two seeds with dominant traits (RRYY) and non-dominant traits (rryy) resulted in the production of 4 types of gametes (RY, Ry, rY and ry). This means each of the gametes segregates independently of the other, and each with a frequency of 25% of the total gametes produced.

3. A man with blood group A marries a woman with blood group O, and their daughter has blood group O. Is this information enough to tell you which of the traits – blood group A or O – is dominant? Why or why not?

Solution: Given information is not enough to tell us which characteristics are dominant – blood group A or O. Blood type A is always dominant in ABO blood, and blood type O is always recessive. Here, the father’s blood group may be genotypically AA (homozygous) or AO (heterozygous), whereas that of the mother can be OA or OO.

4. How is the sex of the child determined in human beings?

Solution: The sex of the child in humans is determined by the males. Males have XY chromosomes, while females have XX chromosomes. Hence, if the male’s X chromosome combines with the female’s X chromosome, the child is a girl; if the male’s Y chromosome combines with the female’s X chromosome, the child is a boy.

Exercise-9.3 Page: 150

1. What are the different ways in which individuals with a particular trait may increase in a population?

Solution: An individual attribute could increase a population in the following 2 ways: (a) Natural selection: If an attribute is useful to a population, it’ll increase naturally. For example, mosquitoes which are resilient against a particular pesticide will pass on their genes so that future generations become resistant as well. The mosquitoes which are affected by the pesticide die out. (b) Genetic drift: If a species faces a catastrophic event where most of the population is wiped out, the surviving population can pass on their traits to the following generations. This may result in a rise in the attribute within the population.

2. Why are traits acquired during the lifetime of an individual not inherited?

Solution: Traits acquired during a lifetime cannot be inherited by successive generations as the changes do not reflect in the DNA of the germ cells. For instance, a football player cannot pass on his skills to his offspring as they are limited to non-reproductive cells only.

3. Why are the small numbers of surviving tigers a cause of worry from the point of view of genetics?

Solution: As the size of the tiger population decreases, the genetic pool of the species decreases too. This results in a limitation on the variations which will be introduced within the genetic makeup of the tigers. This lack of variation will result in serious implications. For example, if an illness spreads within the tiger population, it can potentially wipe out the whole population, possibly causing their extinction.

Exercise–9.4 Page: 151

1. What factors could lead to the rise of a new species?

Solution: Mutation, genetic drift, natural selection, geographical isolation, reproductive isolation for prolonged periods, environmental factors on isolated populations, and the quantum of genetic variation transmissible from one generation to the following generation.

2. Will geographical isolation be a major factor in the speciation of a self-pollinating plant species? Why or why not?

Solution: In self-pollinating plant species, geographical isolation is usually not a major factor, as no new trait will become part of the genetic makeup of a self-pollinating plant species. However, environmental changes may result in some variations.

3. Will geographical isolation be a major factor in the speciation of an organism that reproduces asexually? Why or why not?

Solution: In asexually reproducing organisms, geographical isolation cannot be considered a major factor because meiosis does not occur during asexual modes of reproduction.

Exercise–9.5 Page: 156

1. Give an example of characteristics being used to determine how close two species are in evolutionary terms.

Solution: Humans and chimpanzees share many characteristics and a very high percentage of DNA. This indicates that humans and chimpanzees shared a common ancestor long ago, and chimpanzees are among the closest living relatives of humans.

2. Can the wing of a butterfly and the wing of a bat be considered homologous organs? Why or why not?

Solution: No. Although both structures help in flying, they have evolved separately. Butterfly wings are made of chitinous membranes, whereas bat wings have a bony skeleton with blood vessels. They are analogous organs.

3. What are fossils? What do they tell us about the process of evolution?

Solution: Fossils are the preserved remains of animals, plants, or other organisms that died millions of years ago. They provide information about extinct organisms, their structure and way of life, evolutionary relationships, and the time when different species were formed or became extinct.

Exercise–9.6 Page: 158

1. Why are human beings who look so different from each other in terms of size, colour and looks said to belong to the same species?

Solution: Human beings belong to the same species because they have the same basic genetic makeup and can interbreed to produce fertile offspring, even though they vary in colour, size and appearance.

2. In evolutionary terms, can we say which among bacteria, spiders, fish and chimpanzees have a ‘better’ body design? Why or why not?

Solution: No. Body designs are the result of environmental needs and pressure. A fish has a streamlined body suited to water, while a spider or chimpanzee has a body suited to its own environment. Therefore, no organism can be called universally better.

Exercises Page: 159

1. A Mendelian experiment consisted of breeding tall pea plants bearing violet flowers with short pea plants bearing white flowers. The progeny all bore violet flowers, but almost half of them were short. This suggests that the genetic makeup of the tall parent can be depicted as (a) TTWW (b) TTww (c) TtWW (d) TtWw

Solution: Correct answer – (c) TtWW. The tall parent must carry the gene for shortness because almost half the progeny are short, while all progeny have violet flowers, showing that violet is dominant.

2. An example of homologous organs is (a) Our arm and a dog’s foreleg (b) Our teeth and an elephant’s tusks (c) Potato and runners of grass (d) All of the above

Solution: Correct answer – (d). Homologous organs have the same basic origin or structural plan but perform different functions.

3. In evolutionary terms, we have more in common with (a) A Chinese schoolboy (b) A chimpanzee (c) A spider (d) A bacterium

Solution: Correct answer – (a). A Chinese schoolboy is also a human being belonging to the same species, Homo sapiens.

4. A study found that children with light-coloured eyes are likely to have parents with light-coloured eyes. On this basis, can we say anything about whether the light eye colour trait is dominant or recessive? Why or why not?

Solution: No. Knowledge of at least three generations is required to determine whether a trait is dominant or recessive.

5. How are the areas of study – evolution and classification – interlinked?

Solution: Evolution explains how organisms change over time, while classification groups organisms according to similarities and relationships. Classification therefore reflects evolutionary relationships.

6. Explain the terms analogous and homologous organs with examples.

Solution: Homologous organs have the same basic structure and origin but different functions, such as the forelimbs of humans and wings of bats. Analogous organs have different structures and origins but similar functions, such as the wings of birds and insects.

7. Outline a project which aims to find the dominant coat colour in dogs.

Solution: Select dogs with contrasting coat colours, such as a homozygous black dog (BB) and a homozygous brown dog (bb). Cross them and observe the offspring. If all offspring are black, black is likely dominant. Cross the heterozygous offspring (Bb) and observe the next generation. A 3:1 black-to-brown ratio would support black being dominant.

8. Explain the importance of fossils in deciding evolutionary relationships.

Solution: Fossils provide evidence about organisms, their structure, behaviour and evolutionary history. They help scientists compare extinct organisms with living organisms and understand how groups such as whales evolved from land-dwelling ancestors.

9. What evidence do we have for the origin of life from inanimate matter?

Solution: Stanley L. Miller and Harold C. Urey’s 1953 experiment simulated the early Earth’s atmosphere using gases such as ammonia and hydrogen. Electric sparks represented lightning. The experiment produced amino acids, showing that organic molecules necessary for life could form from non-living substances.

10. Explain how sexual reproduction gives rise to more viable variations than asexual reproduction. How does this affect the evolution of those organisms that reproduce sexually?

Solution: Sexual reproduction produces variations through errors in DNA copying, random segregation of chromosomes, exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes, and the combination of genes from two parents. Asexual reproduction usually involves one parent and produces genetically similar offspring. Greater variation helps sexually reproducing organisms adapt and evolve more effectively.

11. How is the equal genetic contribution of male and female parents ensured in the progeny?

Solution: Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes. The female contributes 22 autosomes and one X chromosome, while the male contributes 22 autosomes and either an X or a Y chromosome. Thus, the zygote receives equal numbers of chromosomes from both parents.

12. Only variations that confer an advantage to an individual organism will survive in a population. Do you agree with this statement? Why or why not?

Solution: No, the statement is not completely correct. Variations that are advantageous are more likely to help organisms survive and reproduce, but neutral variations may also remain in a population. Harmful variations are generally selected against. For example, heat-resistant bacteria may survive better when the temperature rises.
